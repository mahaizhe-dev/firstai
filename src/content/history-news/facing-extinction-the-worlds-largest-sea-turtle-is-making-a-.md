The world’s largest sea turtle may be adapting to climate change by establishing new nesting grounds along Ecuador’s cooler coastline, where researchers have recorded unprecedented nesting activity and promising hatching rates. The discovery offers a rare glimmer of hope for a critically endangered population that has declined by more than 90% since the 1980s.
